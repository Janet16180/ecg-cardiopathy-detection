# Affordable next ECG model ideas: research review

**24 September 2026. All four proposals are now user-authorized queue entries 014–017; implementation and results are pending.** Prepared by the requested Astra research agent after reading the experiment queue, findings, original papers and authors' repositories. See the [persistent queue](experiment-queue.md) for current cost-based priority: 014 fusion, 015 distillation, 017 morphology templates, then 016 xECG probe/fine-tune. The research ranking below reflects expected scientific utility as well as cost; it is not the execution order. No training was launched by this review.

The best immediate practical bet is **combining the existing ECG-JEPA and CPC predictors using development patients**. The most useful representation experiment is **transferring the already cached JEPA representations into the compact CPC encoder**. For an architectural contribution, test **a time-preserving morphology-template branch beside the existing CPC path**, with an ordinary convolution branch as the crucial control. A short xECG optimization experiment is also justified; another large xECG SSL adaptation is not the affordable first choice.

## What the literature and our results actually establish

The xECG paper is now a peer-reviewed early-access article in *npj Digital Medicine*, published **14 September 2026**. It reports the leading aggregate BenchECG score, 0.838, across its heterogeneous task suite. That score is not the AUROC of our binary endpoint. Its reported training acceleration is relative to the authors' transformer settings, not a V100 timing guarantee. [Published paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03196-y), [official code](https://github.com/dlaskalab/bench-xecg), [released checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/riccardolunelli/xECG_base_model_v1).

A different ICLR 2026 benchmark covers 12 datasets and 26 tasks, and finds different strengths across ECG-JEPA, ECGFounder and ECG-CPC. Its ECG-CPC is a structured state-space model, not our local CNN/GRU implementation. Code and checkpoint instructions are public. This supports retaining several strong references rather than declaring a universal winner. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25095), [authors' repository](https://github.com/AI4HealthUOL/ecg-fm-benchmarking).

The particular released ECG-JEPA used here is **Kim's “Learning General Representation of 12-Lead Electrocardiogram…”**, with Cross-Pattern Attention. It must not be confused with the separate paper titled “Self-Supervised Pre-Training with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture Boosts ECG Classification Performance.” Kim's paper has an April 2026 revision; code and pretrained checkpoints are available. Our local checkpoint hash and preprocessing are in [JEPA notes](jepa-notes.md). [Correct paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08559), [official code/checkpoints](https://github.com/sehunfromdaegu/ECG_JEPA).

Our evidence favors exploiting existing representations before scaling training: limited-label CPC improved approximately 0.910 to 0.934 AUROC; full-label compact CPC variants reached approximately 0.955–0.957; frozen released JEPA reached 0.9545 with full labels. Ordinary local CPC features beat prediction-mismatch features. Chunk compression, heartbeat boundaries and cluster objectives have not surpassed ordinary native-grid CPC. xECG selected very early checkpoints and then deteriorated on development despite decreasing training loss; its cause remains undiagnosed. These are exploratory results on an already inspected test cohort, not established differences between architectures. [Findings](model-findings-report.md), [004](../outputs/experiment004_cpc_40k/report.md), [006](../outputs/experiment006_cpc_tokenization/report.md), [007](../outputs/experiment007_xecg/report.md), [009](../outputs/experiment009_cpc_prediction_mismatch/report.md).

## Ranked comparison

Times below are **inferred planning ranges for a bounded pilot**, not measurements of these proposed methods. They include a need to measure loading and evaluation. Any GPU pilot must pass a real end-to-end profile and a total-budget gate before launch.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | JEPA + CPC fusion from saved features/scores | Practical ensemble: do their errors complement each other? | Each constituent, fixed equal blend, five-weight development-selected blend | Approximately 1–15 CPU minutes if saved probes/caches verify; additional waveform inference only if needed | Strongly correlated errors; selecting a blend on too few patients |
| 2 | Cached JEPA-to-CPC representation distillation | Representation/training: can a small raw-waveform model absorb useful pretrained structure? | Identical CPC initialization with and without teacher-feature loss | Target 15–60 minutes for two short arms; conditional on cold-data profile | Teacher provenance/overlap, incompatible invariances, suppressing useful CPC features |

This ranking values probability of a useful result per unit of work. Rank 4 is the most direct architecture study; rank 1 may improve a system without improving any individual encoder. None is a verified novelty claim.

## 1. Combine complementary existing models before retraining them

**Mechanism.** A good temporal CNN/GRU and a large pretrained JEPA need not rank the same borderline ECGs incorrectly. Averaging can reduce those errors if they are complementary. Ensemble compression is an established motivation for knowledge distillation; neither the ensemble nor its compression is a new concept. [Hinton, Vinyals and Dean, 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531).

**Immediate cache-only pilot.** Reconstruct logits from the saved JEPA linear probe and Experiment 009's ordinary local-plus-context CPC probe. The latter is a weaker individual predictor, but testing whether it adds information is cheap. Both feature caches cover 19,126 eligible recordings. Join by ECG ID, patient ID and partition; do not assume row order. Use the respective full-label or fixed 1,518-label probes, never a full-label teacher in a limited-label comparison.

Normalize each predictor's raw logits using its training-patient mean and standard deviation. Evaluate `alpha * JEPA + (1-alpha) * CPC` for a frozen grid `alpha = {0, .25, .5, .75, 1}`. The endpoints retain the individual models, and `.5` is the untuned ensemble comparator. Select the weight on development only, then fit a fresh calibration transform and threshold on calibration patients. Do not search weights on existing test predictions. No stacking model is needed. If later using a trainable stacking head, fit it with out-of-fold training predictions rather than in-sample predictions from supervised encoders.

**Stronger follow-up, only if useful.** Regenerate development predictions from the saved end-to-end CPC checkpoint and repeat the same prespecified small blend grid. The frozen CPC probe is not a substitute for that stronger practical comparator. Checkpoint choice must follow each experiment's development selection; avoid choosing among CPC variants by their historical test scores.

**Go/no-go.** Advance only if the blend improves development specificity in the high-sensitivity region, without more than 0.002 AUROC loss, and its benefit is not confined to one tiny subgroup or one weight. A practical pilot signal is at least +0.02 absolute specificity; this is a decision threshold, not a significance claim. If endpoints win or the selected interior weight is unstable under patient resampling, stop. A two-encoder deployment increases inference cost, so keep its gain and cost explicit.

## 2. Transfer cached JEPA features into the compact raw-waveform encoder

**Evidence.** TinyMIM demonstrated that distillation can make small vision models benefit from representations learned by large masked-modeling teachers. It also found token-relation targets stronger than simple feature targets in its setting. That motivates evaluating the target, not assuming any feature loss must work. Its released code/checkpoints are for images. [CVPR 2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01296), [official code](https://github.com/OliverRensu/TinyMIM).

**Proposed representation experiment.** Reuse the frozen JEPA global vectors already cached for the **15,360 eligible training records**, including hidden labels in the limited-label setting. The initial distillation subset is therefore a subset of the frozen 56,875-record pool, not all of it. Report this accurately; do not imply that MIMIC teacher vectors already exist. All arms use precisely that same subset. Later expansion to the rest of the pool requires new extraction, a separate cost estimate and a matched continuation control.

Start both students from the same ordinary CPC SSL checkpoint, preserving its twelve-lead input, stem, chronology, readout and normalization. Add the same small projection from the pooled student representation to 768 dimensions in both arms. Compare supervised BCE alone with BCE plus a fixed `0.1 * (1 - cosine(projected_student, frozen_teacher))` term. Apply BCE only where the designated label budget exposes a label. Match batches, labeled-example exposures, optimizer updates and initialization; compute teacher targets from a cache, with no teacher GPU forward pass during training. The projection is discarded at inference. Teacher targets are deterministic full-record targets; waveform augmentations that alter semantics should not be added to just one arm.

The BCE-only control runs the same sample schedule, including unlabeled examples, so a gain does not come from more labeled updates. Record both useful optimizer work and total wall time. A later small shuffled-target control can help distinguish teacher information from generic regularization, but should follow a promising pilot rather than multiplying the initial suite. This modifies the training objective while holding the student architecture fixed. It is not a fair architecture ranking against the teacher, which has external pretraining exposure.

**Why this could help here.** It transfers a representation that already works locally into a model that already trains effectively locally. Binary soft-label distillation contains little task information beyond one probability; the 768-dimensional teacher vector can transfer richer structure without exposing hidden diagnostic labels. This is a hypothesis. Global targets cannot teach event timing directly, and teacher invariance may erase amplitude or transient features useful to our endpoint.

**Go/no-go.** A two-arm pilot with a short fixed update budget should show development improvement against the same resumed CPC, not simply low feature-reconstruction loss. Stop if downstream discrimination drops, student variance collapses, or no improvement survives a second matched seed. If feature distillation fails, it does not invalidate relational or token distillation; those alternatives require extra teacher extraction and alignment and are not automatically cheap. Preserve the released teacher's uncertain pretraining-overlap caveat.

## 3. Give xECG a trained classifier before adjusting its representation

**Evidence and scope.** Kumar et al. showed in vision that initializing a pretrained model with a random classifier can distort features during fine-tuning; linear probing followed by fine-tuning improved their transfer results. Their theory and experiments do not diagnose our xECG run or establish that our development patients constitute their kind of distribution shift. [ICLR 2022 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054), [official implementation](https://github.com/AnanyaKumar/transfer_learning).

**Minimal study.** Extract mean-pooled features once from the original released xECG checkpoint, and fit its linear classifier using only training labels with the existing development regularization grid. Retain that frozen probe as arm A. Arm B starts the same released encoder with the original random classifier. Arm C starts the same encoder with the trained classifier. B and C receive the exact same two-epoch full-label fine-tuning schedule, backbone/head learning rates, batch order, effective batch and checkpoint-selection times. Start with the existing 007 optimizer settings; lowering only C's learning rate would confound initialization with optimization. Reset optimizer state in both arms.

If the probe uses standardized features, fold the scaler into the affine weights before transferring them: `w_raw = w / scale`, `b_raw = b - dot(w_raw, mean)`. Verify logits before unfreezing. This avoids silently changing the starting predictor. Initialize the native PyTorch head with the matching shape and bias conventions.

**Go/no-go.** First measure the frozen probe. If it already performs strongly, keeping it frozen may be the appropriate result. Proceed with the short B/C comparison if the end-to-end profile fits the budget. A development improvement of C over B is evidence about this transfer recipe, not proof of the feature-distortion mechanism. If both decline, stop the expensive continuation and inspect train/development feature movement and loss. Do not revive 008 based on that failure.

WiSE-FT is related but less direct here: its original setting interpolates compatible zero-shot and fine-tuned predictors. Our released ECG encoder lacks a pretrained binary proxy head, so blindly interpolating its encoder with a separately fitted head is not an exact WiSE-FT reproduction. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903).

## 4. Preserve the native grid and add explicit morphology matching

**Source idea.** ShapeFormer combines discriminative shapelet features with generic sequence features. Its KDD 2024 study covers 30 UEA datasets, and the public repository includes AtrialFibrillation; this is already related to physiological time series and must not be sold as the first application of shapelets to ECG. Its training procedure includes label-dependent shapelet discovery and best-fit subsequence selection. Code is available, but no pretrained twelve-lead checkpoint suitable for our proxy was verified. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14608), [authors' code](https://github.com/xuanmay2701/shapeformer).

**Our adaptation.** Keep the complete native CPC stream and its existing 250 Hz input. Alongside it, compute distance responses to a small bank of twelve-lead waveform templates at every existing token time, then concatenate or add a projected template response before the unchanged context/readout. Use one fixed support initially, for example 200 ms, and 32 templates. At 250 Hz this is a `32 x 12 x 50` bank. Align each response to the right edge of the corresponding causal token support and respect each five-second half boundary. This prevents future leakage if later used for CPC. Preserve amplitude using the existing train-only scaling rather than normalizing every local window to unit norm.

Squared distances can be evaluated with convolutions and sliding sums, avoiding materializing a large array of waveform windows. Start templates from a seed-fixed sample of training-only windows; no outcome-guided discovery in this first pilot. Project responses through a small nonlinear branch, with a zero-initialized final projection so the common CPC checkpoint is initially unchanged. Earlier branch layers are nonzero. The baseline has an ordinary convolution bank with the same shape, training-window initialization, projection, nonlinearity and normalization. A no-branch model provides the practical reference.

**Critical interpretation.** Squared template distance expands into a dot product, window energy and template norm. It is therefore closely related to a convolution with energy-dependent terms. A win over no branch alone may only reflect extra capacity. Template distance versus the matched convolution branch tests the useful contribution of the distance formulation; it does not establish a wholly new mathematical operator. If desired later, add a convolution-plus-window-energy control to determine whether explicit matching adds anything beyond that algebraic structure.

**Why this differs from 006/009/012.** It keeps every native token instead of replacing the sequence with beat or learned chunks; computes ordinary waveform similarity rather than future-prediction error; and changes the local input representation rather than the long-range context operator. The branch can represent a brief local pattern while the original path retains timing and context. A small supervised experiment from the same CPC initialization is the first test; full SSL retraining is unnecessary to screen the idea.

**Go/no-go.** Compare the template and convolution branches under the same fixed short supervised schedule at both existing label budgets. Require a benefit over the convolution branch, not only no-branch CPC. Reject an apparent gain dominated by clipping, amplitude outliers or a single training template/patient. Template matches are not validated explanations or diagnoses. This is a compact ShapeFormer-inspired adaptation, not reproduction of its complete dual-transformer architecture.

A wall-time ceiling must save recovery state and preserve the GPU lock protocol. Cached feature work should use CPU and avoid disturbing the active GPU runner or downloader.

## Common evaluation contract

Use the frozen pool and patient partitions: 56,875 available training waveforms; full 15,360 or fixed 1,518 exposed training labels; 1,306 development, 564 calibration and 1,896 retained test recordings. Some proposals deliberately use a documented training-only subset for a cheaper pilot. No train-only scaler, template selection, teacher fitting or distillation target construction may consume development/calibration/test labels. Never transfer a full-label supervised teacher into a claimed 1,518-label comparison.

Use development patients for all cheap go/no-go screening. For operating-point screening, split development by patient into fixed folds: choose the 95%-sensitivity threshold on the other development folds and report specificity and attained sensitivity on the held-out fold. This avoids tuning and reporting the threshold on the same development patients. AUROC uses held-out development predictions. A bootstrap can describe instability but does not substitute for a second training seed or a fresh external cohort.

Before final evaluation, freeze the model, combination weight, checkpoints, training budget and comparison. Fit Platt calibration and select the highest threshold attaining at least 95% sensitivity on calibration patients only. Primary practical outcome is **test specificity at that frozen threshold, accompanied by attained test sensitivity**; calibration targeting 95% does not guarantee it on test. Report AUROC, AP, Brier, ECE and paired patient-bootstrap differences. A specificity improvement bought by a sensitivity drop is not an equal-operating-point improvement. Include development high-sensitivity partial ROC behavior as supporting evidence, not a second opportunity to pick the winner after test inspection.

A candidate should show a repeatable development signal before test evaluation, with no material AUROC loss and no calibration failure. Report every tried arm and seed, parameters, label exposures, updates, input/pretraining differences, actual end-to-end wall time and memory. The existing test has already informed this research program; further scores remain exploratory. The endpoint is an ECG diagnostic annotation proxy, not confirmed health, cardiopathy in a person, or validated university referral need.

## Other reviewed directions and recommendation

TimeMixer++ (ICLR 2025) uses multiresolution time imaging and mixing across temporal scales/periods. It is a legitimate independent architecture source, but a ten-second ECG with changing rhythm and brief events is not obviously well served by dominant-period folding. Given 006's unfavorable compression results, it ranks below keeping the native stream with a local residual branch. [Official proceedings paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/2b187165e28fdfdc0ffb34d1bfff2b0c-Abstract-Conference.html).

KDA/CKDA, StripedHyena-inspired mixing and Mamba-3 remain the authorized architecture queue; this review does not repackage them as new proposals. Frozen smooth/max readout and the xECG boundary-spanning stem are already documented in [xECG next experiments](xecg-next-experiments.md), so they are not counted as fresh ideas here.

**Recommended next research decision:** run the cache-only fusion screen first if selected, then a bounded cached-teacher distillation pilot. If the aim is a new architectural contribution, prioritize the template-versus-convolution residual comparison while continuing the already authorized 011 implementation separately. xECG LP-FT is a focused optimization check worth doing only after its frozen probe and full data-path timing establish that it fits the available budget.
