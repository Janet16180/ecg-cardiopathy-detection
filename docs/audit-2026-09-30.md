### Challenge label mapping (docs/challenge-label-mapping.md, ecg_experiment/challenge_labels.py)

Verdict: sound with caveats. The caveats are about validity, not bugs.

Checks done
- The rule in the doc (lines 35-40) matches the code at `challenge_labels.py:177-179`:
  - primary negative only when the set of code groups is exactly `{sinus_rhythm}`;
  - codes in neither official table become `unknown`, which gives NaN.
- The per-source counts agree with the downstream manifests:
  - Ningbo primary negatives: 4,542 raw, and 4,528 after cleaning;
  - Chapman negatives: 1,366 in the mapping, and 820 + 273 + 273 = 1,366 in the split.
- Official table hashes: `metadata.json.official_mapping_sha256` equals doc lines 17-18.

Issues
- Medium, validity (label definition). The primary negative is sinus rhythm as the only code. That is stricter than PTB-XL NORM or SPH code 1, and it leaves half the records undefined:
  - Ningbo 17,760 of 34,903;
  - Chapman 5,151 of 10,247;
  - see line 74.
  Every record whose only abnormality is a rhythm or form code (AF, flutter, PVCs, axis, LV high voltage, paced) is dropped from evaluation. So Challenge evaluations compare "superclass-coded abnormal" against "pristine sinus rhythm". That is an easier contrast than a real screening population, and it is the likely reason in-family AUROCs reach 0.99 in 022b (`result.json ranking.xecg.evaluations["family:chapman_ningbo"].metrics.pooled.auroc = 0.9905`). The Challenge-family AUROCs should not be compared directly with SPH or PTB-XL.
- Medium, validity (label definition). The label definitions differ across sources:
  - CPSC 2018 codes only nine classes, so it has no MI or HYP positives (line 76);
  - CPSC-Extra has no negatives at all;
  - Ningbo codes no AF;
  - the handling of LV high voltage (4,106 Ningbo records moved to undefined) is a judgment call with an unapplied alternative (line 61).
  Pooling these sources pools different definitions of "positive".
- Low. The about 1,900 records in line 61 could not be verified; the doc gives no source for the number.

### Cohorts v2 (docs/clean-cohorts-v2.md)

Verdict: sound with caveats. The caveat below is a latent leakage hazard and should be fixed before any pretraining on v2.

Checks done
- The tier compositions add up: 25k is 17,405 + 3,986 + 1,231 + 1,227 + 789 + 362 = 25,000, and 100k adds up to 100,000.
- The manifest source counts match the table.
- CODE-15 has 127,268 exams in the cohorts, against 127,270 usable. The 2 removed repeated waveforms explain the difference (line 104).
- Built at 03:01-03:03, before the Challenge split v1 (03:28).
- `git grep` shows no runner or script on origin/main that reads a `clean_*_v2` manifest. Only notebook 10 does, so no experiment in scope used these cohorts.

Issues
- High, latent leakage hazard. The v2 pretraining cohorts contain Challenge records from every split group, because they were built before the split and never consult it. I joined `train_manifest.csv` to `challenge_splits_v1/rows.csv`:
  - `clean_25k_v2` holds 1,512 test-group and 1,528 calibration-group Challenge records;
  - `clean_100k_v2` holds 12,603 test-group and 12,597 calibration-group records.
  An encoder pretrained on any v2 tier would have seen the Challenge test waveforms without labels. Its later Challenge calibration or test readout would then not be clean. Neither the doc nor the split doc mentions this. It is not a problem for any finished experiment, because none used v2. It must be fixed, by filtering to `split == "train"` or by writing an explicit dated rule, before a CPC/SSL run on v2 is evaluated on Challenge groups.
- Low. Decision 1 in lines 145-146 (whether N counts all records) is still unconfirmed by the user.

## Programme-level assessment

### Solid

- The released frozen encoders (xECG, ECG-JEPA) beat the project's CPC by about 0.035-0.04 AUROC on PTB-XL
  development, SPH and EchoNext, with the same sign on every set and budget. This is the best-supported
  finding in the programme.
- Ranking transfers to SPH (0.911-0.915 for a PTB-XL-only readout), while a threshold and calibration carried
  from PTB-XL do not (027: sensitivity about 0.92 instead of 0.95, calibration intercept −1.1 to −1.9). 027 is
  the cleanest experiment in the set.
- On EchoNext, ECG features beat cart measurements plus demographics (+0.075 [+0.063, +0.086] for CPC), and an
  ECG-abnormality head is a poor structural-disease detector (about 0.75). EchoNext validation was read once
  for 023 and again for 028 only.
- The data pipeline: manifests, counts and hashes reproduce, duplicate groups do not cross split groups, and
  the per-record limitation of the Challenge split is disclosed.

### Fragile

- Everything that was selected on SPH: the 022b pooled readout (0.939), the 026b multi-hospital normal
  reference (0.878), the 033 finding heads, the 035 reweighting "with no SPH cost" and 037's adoption. SPH has
  been read by about 16 of these experiments and used to choose between arms in at least five. Its numbers are
  now development numbers.
- 035's fix and 037's adoption: 15 arms compared on 41 hard-subset positives with no multiplicity correction,
  then adopted although v2 is worse on 037's primary endpoint (−0.0048 [−0.0072, −0.0025]) and fails the
  strict non-inferiority reading. The rule followed was literally met because adoption was the default.
- The operating-point numbers prepared for the cardiologist (29/30/033/037): one fixed SPH local/evaluation
  split, the local site equal to the evaluation site (the most favourable case), and "other" ECGs not counted
  as false referrals.
- Rare-finding sensitivities: WPW, AV block and long QT at SPH rest on 15-27 positives each; the AF and AV block
  AUROC of 0.9999 reflects easy hospital cases (21 of 27 AV blocks are complete heart block), not the subtle
  pre-excitation or borderline QTc a student screen needs.
- Anything read off the Challenge families (leave-one-source-out, calibration groups): the split is per record
  with no patient IDs, JEPA and xECG were pretrained on Chapman and Ningbo waveforms, and xECG on full CODE
  (so CODE-15 is not unseen for it either).
- All results are one fit per head, one seed, with fits held fixed in the bootstrap; intervals describe
  evaluation sampling only.

### The three biggest risks to the thesis claims

1. There is no untouched confirmatory test set left for the adopted pipeline. The PTB-XL test cohort that
   pipeline-v2.md offers as a final test was already examined by Experiments 001-008 (docs/experiment-003-mimic.md:63,
   "37 earlier evaluated runs"; 001 and 007 report frozen JEPA and xECG test AUROCs on it), and the choice of
   xECG/JEPA followed those readings. SPH is spent. The Challenge test groups remain unscored, but they are
   per-record (same-patient leakage possible), come from sources the encoders were pretrained on, and are
   already embedded in features_challenge_v1 and included in the cohorts v2 pretraining tiers
   (1,512 test-group records in clean_25k_v2, 12,603 in clean_100k_v2). Any future SSL model trained on v2
   would have seen them.
2. The label is not the clinical target, and it differs between sources. The binary endpoint is a PTB-XL
   superclass proxy; the Challenge negative is "sinus rhythm as the only code", about half of Ningbo/Chapman is
   undefined, CPSC has no MI/HYP positives and CPSC-Extra no negatives. 025b's below-chance CPC scores on the
   PTB-XL hard subset and 035's "normal-label mismatch" are symptoms. None of it is the athlete criteria the
   student screen needs, and no experiment includes a single student ECG or a young-subgroup readout.
3. Decisions compound on reused, single-fit evidence. Each adoption (022b, 026b, 033, 035, 037) was made on
   SPH or on the PTB-XL development set that has been examined many times, with one seed, and each later
   experiment builds on the previous winner. Small gains (+0.004 to +0.02) and the v2 choice could be selection
   noise, and the summary documents already present them as settled ("adopted", "expected operating numbers").

### What a skeptical examiner would ask for

- A fresh, patient-level held-out set never used for any selection, ideally from a site whose waveforms none of
  the encoders saw in pretraining, scored once with v1 and v2 side by side. Failing that, an explicit
  statement that the PTB-XL test cohort is exploratory.
- Seed and refit variance: at least three seeds, or bootstraps that refit the heads, for every adopted arm.
- Remove Challenge calibration/test records from cohorts v2 before any pretraining run on them.
- A young-subgroup (18-35) readout on PTB-XL development and SPH, with the student label relabelled by the
  cardiologist's criteria on a sample.
- Referral load that counts every non-abnormal ECG (including "other" rhythm and axis statements) as a false
  referral, and results for a local site different from the evaluation site.
- Confidence intervals on every rare-finding sensitivity (WPW, AV block, long QT), and a correction for the 15
  arms in 035.
- A table of every read of SPH, PTB-XL development and the Challenge calibration groups, with the decision
  each read fed.
