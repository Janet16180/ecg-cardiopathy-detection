## Question and fixed comparison

How well does the [authors' released xECG encoder](https://huggingface.co/riccardolunelli/xECG_base_model_v1) transfer to our existing PTB-XL binary `diagnostic_abnormality` proxy with all eligible public training labels and with the fixed 10% label manifest? The two arms use the **same pretrained encoder** and differ only in label budget:

| Arm | Eligible training ECG labels | Fixed evaluation cohorts |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Full labels | 15,360 | Development 1,306; calibration 564; test 1,896 |
| 10% manifest | 1,518 | Same patients and ECGs |

Use seed 42. Train each arm for at most 40 supervised epochs, with patience 8, selecting the checkpoint by development AUROC. Use the existing patient-exclusive split and the existing 10% patient selection; do not resample the labeled subset. Start with FP32 on the V100, microbatch 16 and four-step gradient accumulation for an effective batch of 64. Use a one-epoch learning-rate warmup followed by cosine decay. Follow the authors' [PTB-XL configuration](https://github.com/dlaskalab/bench-xecg/tree/release/configs) for encoder learning rate 3e-5, head learning rate 1e-3, weight decay 0.1, layerwise decay 0.75, and drop-path 0.5, subject to a successful backend/real-batch check. Keep these choices fixed across the two arms. Fit Platt calibration and choose the threshold on the separate calibration patients under the current project's at-least-95%-sensitivity rule. Freeze that threshold before evaluating the existing test cohort. Report AUROC, average precision, Brier score, sensitivity, specificity, precision, confusion matrix, and paired patient-bootstrap intervals where available. Record class counts and coverage of the proxy label. The protocol is a practical transfer comparison, not a reproduction of BenchECG's five-class PTB-XL task.

## Checkpoint and input contract

Use the official `riccardolunelli/xECG_base_model_v1` release, rather than a randomly initialized xLSTM. The [model card](https://huggingface.co/riccardolunelli/xECG_base_model_v1) and [files](https://huggingface.co/riccardolunelli/xECG_base_model_v1/tree/main) identify a 57-million-parameter encoder and a 228 MB `model.safetensors` checkpoint under MIT. The verified weight file is **228,099,912 bytes**, SHA256 `812dec69ac0fbf13f39e50bde4f35f85435a66966d8785baec65c2b5c70e722c`, at Hugging Face revision `b06053253a9bd21445d8189b21813484929a3ffa`. The authors recommend the simplified Hugging Face `xECG.py` and provide a pooled classification wrapper in `downstream_models.py`. Load the pretrained encoder strictly, add an identity-normalized, one-logit linear head, and train with `BCEWithLogitsLoss`. Record unexpected or missing checkpoint keys and stop if encoder weights are not transferred as intended. Initialize the new head deterministically with seed 42.

Follow the [current published methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03196-y_reference.pdf) and the [released code](https://huggingface.co/riccardolunelli/xECG_base_model_v1/blob/main/xECG.py): use the entire ten-second raw waveform in physical mV, resampled from the source's 500 Hz to 100 Hz with anti-alias filtering, without per-lead normalization or other filtering. Preserve the model card's lead order **I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, V6** and a tensor shaped `[batch, time, 12]`. Ten seconds gives 1,000 samples, or 40 patches of 25 samples. Verify waveform units and lead order from the source records. Do not reuse the CPC half-window cache or its training-fitted normalization as xECG input. Reject nonfinite or malformed signals before training and report any exclusions rather than silently changing cohort membership.

Save SHA256 hashes of the model code, classification wrapper, requirements, adapter, preprocessing code, and input manifests before launch, alongside the verified weight hash and revision above. Save package versions, CUDA/GPU details, and selected checkpoint hash with results. The model card pins PyTorch 2.8.0 and `xlstm` 2.0.4, while the full benchmark repository has newer requirements. Isolate and verify the implementation used here rather than assuming those environments are interchangeable.

## Pretraining exposure and scope

The [2026 article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03196-y_reference.pdf) reports that xECG's self-supervised pretraining used full CODE (8,183,458 recordings), Chapman and Ningbo (45,152), and INCART (75 long recordings). It does **not report PTB-XL or MIMIC-IV-ECG as pretraining sources**. This is a source-list observation, not a record-level proof that the released checkpoint is independent of our PTB cohort; exact cross-dataset overlap has not been audited. CODE-15% is part of full CODE and would not be an unseen pretraining holdout.

Our PTB-XL test cohort has already been examined in previous experiments. Treat Experiment 007's result as exploratory and avoid fresh-holdout, prospective, clinical referral, or university-student generalization claims. A single seed does not measure retraining variation. There is **no from-scratch xLSTM arm** here, so any difference from prior models mixes architecture, pretraining corpus, objective, implementation, and fine-tuning choices. It cannot establish an architecture effect or the benefit of pretraining alone.

### Verified implementation and queued execution

Four adapter/preprocessing tests and three runner tests passed, including exact epoch continuation of model, optimizer, scheduler and RNG, and resuming one arm while starting the other. The official encoder strictly loads **57,021,472 parameters**. A real ten-second ECG passed CPU forward and backward with finite loss and gradients. The upstream xLSTM 2.0.4 `vanilla` backend preserves the recurrence; its recurrent kernel and bias layouts are converted from the checkpoint's CUDA storage using the library's conversion functions. Dependencies are isolated in `third_party/xecg-deps`; the running experiments' environment was not upgraded.
