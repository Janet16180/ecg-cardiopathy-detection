# Experiment 016 v9: matched encoder-update intervention

The frozen [protocol](experiment-016-encoder-motion-v9.md) completed as one development-only study on the 15,359-record clean training cohort. Both arms began with identical released xECG weights and the clean C=0.01 probe head. Each made 240 FP32 updates over one epoch, with the same seed-46 record order, full encoder gradients, global clipping at 3.0, head optimizer, and warmup. Only the encoder learning rates differed: historical layerwise rates in Moving (M), exactly zero in Frozen (F). Calibration and test patients were not evaluated.

The prespecified **F joint minus M joint AUROC** was **+0.006067**, above the +0.005 harm screen. Its 2,000-draw paired whole-patient 95% interval was **+0.001696 to +0.010901**; no draw had a single class. The M refit exceeded F refit by **+0.002157** (interval **−0.000127 to +0.004670**). The M readout gap was **+0.007679** and F's was **−0.000546**, yielding the prespecified gap difference **+0.008224** (interval **+0.004030 to +0.012802**). The frozen readout-gap rule therefore passed: the moving encoder retained measured linearly useful features while its jointly trained head discriminated less well at this fixed endpoint. The positive refit difference is a point estimate with an interval crossing zero; it is not proof that encoder adaptation improves held-out-patient performance.

The control is unusually direct. Both arms' first two seed-46 batch digests, gradient norms, clip factors, and head-step magnitudes matched before encoder motion separated their paths. F's entire encoder state remained bitwise unchanged after every update; its final features matched the released feature cache exactly, and its refit logits differed from the clean reference by at most **1.78×10⁻¹⁴**. M's encoder changed after the first nonzero-rate step. Every update clipped in both arms. Median preclip encoder/head norms were **20.06/0.99** for M and **16.12/0.81** for F. Median clip factors were **0.14934** (M) and **0.18589** (F); median head update norms were **0.002481** and **0.003676**. These trajectory differences are downstream mediator evidence, not an isolated cause of the AUROC contrast. The fixed training-only 128-ECG diagnostic had M median feature cosine **0.96273** to release at update 240; F's features matched the release exactly. Per-update norms, learning rates, losses and batch digests, plus all five fixed diagnostic endpoints, are retained in each arm's artifacts.

This identifies a fixed-budget total effect of allowing encoder updates under the matched policy, not encoder drift as the exclusive causal mediator. The moving arm's lower joint-head AUROC is compatible with a readout lag, while optimizer interactions and changes in realized clipping are part of the intervention. A single encoder seed and repeatedly inspected development patients do not establish robustness across checkpoints, tasks, or new patients. The endpoint is an ECG diagnostic annotation proxy, not a clinical referral outcome. The released probe remains the practical reference; no follow-up training, calibration, or test evaluation is authorized by this result.
