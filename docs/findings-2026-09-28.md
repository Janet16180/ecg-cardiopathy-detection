# Findings of 28 September 2026: Experiments 022-027

Six experiments were run on 28 September after the [literature review](literature-review-2026-09-28.md). Each
had a protocol frozen before any score, ran once, and has its own results report (linked below). No PTB-XL
test ECG and no EchoNext test ECG was used. This page brings the results together and states what they mean
for the project. Numbers are AUROC unless stated.

## What we learned

1. **The released frozen encoders are the right base.**
   - ECG-JEPA and xECG beat our CPC in every setting: +0.035-0.04 on PTB-XL (every label budget) and on SPH,
     and +0.011 and +0.026 on EchoNext. All intervals exclude zero.
   - With 100 labels, JEPA and xECG already exceed CPC trained on all 15,359.
   - JEPA and xECG cannot be told apart on PTB-XL. xECG is ahead on EchoNext, and marginally on SPH.
   - Decision: use a frozen xECG or ECG-JEPA with a simple readout as the project baseline.

2. **Rankings generalize to a new hospital; operating points do not.**
   - Discrimination held at SPH, a different country, device and labeling team: JEPA 0.911 and xECG 0.915,
     against 0.936 and 0.939 on PTB-XL full development.
   - A threshold set for 95% sensitivity on PTB-XL gave only about 92% sensitivity at SPH. SPH probabilities
     were also too high (calibration intercept −1.1 to −1.9).
   - Decision: any deployment needs local calibration. How many local labels that takes is now the top backlog
     item.

3. **At 95% sensitivity, screening a healthy population produces many referrals.**
   - At SPH, JEPA and xECG flag about 34-35% of normal ECGs.
   - At an assumed 1% prevalence, only about 3% of referred students would have an abnormal ECG.
   - This is the practical limit to design around: choose the operating point with the clinical team, not by
     AUROC.

4. **The ECG carries echo-confirmed structural heart disease, beyond the cart's measurements.**
   - On EchoNext, CPC features beat the cart's intervals plus demographics by +0.075 [+0.063, +0.086]. xECG plus
     the cart measurements gives 0.840, the best head.
   - LVEF of 45% or below was the easiest component (xECG 0.896). Aortic stenosis was the one component where
     the cart measurements beat CPC.
   - PTB-XL's ECG-abnormality heads applied unchanged reach only about 0.75. An abnormal-ECG detector is not a
     structural-heart-disease detector; the echo target needs its own labels.

5. **A detector trained only on normal ECGs is a credible second signal.**
   - The Mahalanobis distance from normal xECG embeddings reached 0.923 on PTB-XL with no abnormal labels, equal
     to a 100-label classifier.
   - It caught held-out conditions as well as a classifier trained without them: all four superclasses for
     xECG, three of four for JEPA.
   - It lost more at SPH (0.858), so the notion of "normal" also shifts between hospitals.

6. **Reference vectors per condition need a learned space; CPC clusters mostly show devices.**
   - Raw class-mean prototypes trail the probe: −0.077 for CPC, about −0.03 for JEPA and xECG.
   - CPC's unsupervised clusters align more with recording device and heart rate than with diagnosis. 024
     measured this for CPC only; 024b later found JEPA and xECG clusters follow diagnosis more than device
     or hospital.
   - A reference-ECG approach should use JEPA or xECG with a learned projection, and control for device.
   - 024 produced a cardiologist review sheet and 192 candidate label errors.

## Cautions that apply to all of them

- The binary label is an ECG annotation proxy (PTB-XL superclasses, or its SPH mapping), except in EchoNext.
- PTB-XL development patients have been examined many times; SPH and EchoNext validation are the fresher sets.
- Each result is one fit per head, with no second seed.
- SPH is much more heavily filtered than PTB-XL.
- JEPA and xECG were pretrained on Chapman and Ningbo, so those sources cannot serve as external tests for
  them.
- None of this involves students. Prevalence, age and devices at the university will differ.

## Next

The backlog is ranked in [experiment-priorities.md](experiment-priorities.md). The follow-ups these results
added:
- local recalibration (how many site labels restore 95% sensitivity);
- threshold stability;
- a hybrid of the probe and the distance from normal;
- a normal manifold fitted on local ECGs;
- label efficiency for the echo target.

Multi-source training with leave-one-hospital-out evaluation waits for the Ningbo download and the Challenge
label mapping. Ningbo is not expected to change the conclusions above, because the label-efficiency curve is
nearly flat above 4,000 labels. Its likely value is in transfer to other hospitals and calibration across
sites, which are the weak points found here.
