## Question and primary comparison

Does the existing real-data SSL initialization improve responsiveness to a precisely introduced voltage change when localization supervision includes matched edited and control versions of real ECGs? Compare R (original random encoder) with S (existing SSL encoder), both fully trainable with the same original fresh localization head. No additional SSL, checkpoint search, GAN, external data or clinical-label change is part of this study.

The primary endpoint is the paired patient-macro S minus R difference in AP response. Within each positive case, AP response is AP of the edited map against its exact change mask minus AP of its matching control map against that SAME mask. Average focal and repeated response equally within a patient, then average over all 128 evaluation patients. This is counterfactual rank response; a control map with small probabilities can still have high AP because AP measures ordering.

An engineering advance to a separately frozen replication requires ALL of:

- Primary response gain at least 0.02 with paired 95% lower bound strictly above zero.
- Edited-map S minus R AP interval lower bound strictly above −0.01, an absolute engineering noninferiority margin.
- Both R and S have positive 95% lower bounds for within-arm AP response AND target-versus-unedited-candidate probability response.
- S minus R point flag-rate increase at most 0.05 for each of unchanged and added-nuisance controls, under separately calibration-frozen thresholds.

Report every gate and the conjunction. These thresholds do not establish clinical usefulness. Report focal/repeated results separately even if the pooled gate passes; no pooled result claims both modes improve. A failed gate stops advancement without retry, tuning or extra seeds. Patient bootstrap uses 2,000 paired whole-patient draws, seed 20261025, shared interval helpers, 2.5/97.5 percentiles and skipped-draw accounting. The single training seed does not measure optimization variability.
