# Vision self-supervision ideas for raw 12-lead ECG

**Checked:** 24 September 2026. **Status:** research and proposed controlled experiments; no new training result. The practical setting is 56,875 unlabeled training recordings, 10-second 12-lead waveforms, and one V100 with 16 GB. Experiments 005, 006, and 007 are already queued; do not start another GPU job over them.

## Separate encoder from learning objective

xECG's released **57,021,472-parameter bidirectional xLSTM encoder** is an architecture and a set of pretrained weights. **SimDINOv2** is the self-supervised objective used to train it. The [published BenchECG/xECG paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03196-y_reference.pdf), [authors' code](https://github.com/dlaskalab/bench-xecg), and [released model card](https://huggingface.co/riccardolunelli/xECG_base_model_v1) support that distinction. In the local source, `bench_xecg/trainers/ssl_pretrainer.py` computes a teacher/student global embedding loss plus a cosine loss on masked patch embeddings. A vision objective can be adapted to an ECG encoder without importing a giant vision checkpoint or claiming the resulting model is DINOv3, iBOT, or V-JEPA.

The published xECG **0.838 fine-tuned BenchECG aggregate score** supports performance on the paper's benchmark and protocol. It does not predict performance on our binary PTB-XL abnormality proxy, show that xLSTM is superior to another encoder under matched pretraining, or establish benefit from our smaller 56,875-record pool. Experiment 007 tests transfer of released xECG weights. A local objective comparison needs its own matched training controls; the released checkpoint had a different, much larger training corpus, so its downstream score cannot serve as a causal objective control. See [Experiment 007](experiment-007-xecg.md) for preprocessing, overlap, and test-cohort cautions.

## Ranked, bounded candidates

| 1 | **LeJEPA/SIGReg versus SimDINOv2.** [LeJEPA paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544), [author code](https://github.com/galilai-group/lejepa), [xECG source](https://github.com/dlaskalab/bench-xecg). | Train the **same compact** bidirectional xLSTM twice from the same initialization, waveform preprocessing, patient pool, view sampler, updates and examples seen. Existing `pretrain_lejepa.yaml` is **not matched**: it changes width, patch size, and other settings. Local LeJEPA code exists, but requires validation/porting into the controlled setup. |
| 2 | **Dense masked latent prediction, inspired by V-JEPA 2.1.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14482), [official code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2). | Keep the same ECG backbone and training views. Add a small predictor for masked patch teacher embeddings; compare **masked-only versus masked plus visible patch loss** with all else fixed. If useful, test one intermediate-layer auxiliary target in a separate ablation. Mask contiguous time across correlated leads, including the same time region in derived limb leads, to prevent simple cross-lead recovery. | EMA teacher and predictor raise memory/runtime; use a compact backbone, one target layer first, and profile before a full run. Do not copy video/image weights. V-JEPA 2.1's gains are for image/video dense and action tasks, not ECG diagnosis. |
| 3 | **iBOT online masked-patch targets.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07832), [official code](https://github.com/bytedance/ibot). | On the same compact encoder and masks, replace the current patch cosine target with teacher soft-code/distribution prediction while preserving the global objective. Compare to the unmodified patch-cosine arm. Track target entropy, occupied codes, and ECG token variance. | EMA encoder plus projection head; prototype only after candidate 1 or 2 shows an objective gap. A discrete/soft online target can focus on common morphology and miss rare abnormalities. ImageNet accuracy in the paper is evidence for images only. |
| 4 | **DINOv3 Gram anchoring, conditional.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10104), [official code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3). | Monitor patch-embedding geometry and frozen local-feature quality over a long baseline run. Test an anchor only if dense features measurably deteriorate, with the same baseline run length and teacher snapshot. | Low priority here: an additional frozen anchor encoder uses V100 memory. DINOv3's vision advances also rely heavily on large-scale data, optimization, and model scaling; Gram anchoring alone is not a recipe for its performance. |

**I-JEPA and V-JEPA context.** [I-JEPA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Assran_Self-Supervised_Learning_From_Images_With_a_Joint-Embedding_Predictive_Architecture_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf) predicts latent target blocks from visible image context and is a useful design source, but our [ECG-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08559) experiment already supplies a more direct ECG reference. [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985) extends latent prediction to video and reports video/action benchmarks; its large model and robot-action stage do not map directly to 10-second ECGs. The newer verified release is **V-JEPA 2.1** (March 2026, revised June 2026), with dense prediction and deep supervision. I did not verify an official V-JEPA 3 paper/release as of this check, so it is not an experiment dependency. The distinction matters because video temporal prediction and ECG morphology detection have different invariances and signal scales.

## Proposed evaluation gate

**Selected implementation:** [Experiment 008](experiment-008-vision-ssl.md) prioritizes controlled continued adaptation of the already downloaded xECG: a common continuation control, temporal Gram retention, uniform visible-feature retention, and rarity-weighted visible-feature retention. This preserves the existing backbone and avoids changing the regularizer and architecture together. The ranking above records the broader research shortlist; a matched LeJEPA comparison remains future work. Gram retention is being tested as a bounded hypothesis even though dense-feature degradation during our short adaptation has not yet been established. No candidate is a demonstrated improvement for our task.

1. **Finish Experiment 007 first.** Record its released-xECG transfer result as an external reference, not a matched SSL control. Freeze the existing patient split and 10% label manifest before adding new arms.
2. **Preflight candidate 1 with a compact common encoder.** Preserve physical mV, canonical lead order, 100 Hz/1,000-sample input, and the same view augmentation policy. Avoid per-record normalization that removes amplitude information unless both arms explicitly test it. Use the same parameter count, initialization, number of exposures, optimizer updates, supervised probe/fine-tuning code, and classifier initialization. Report actual wall time and peak GPU memory beside quality metrics. A full 57M xLSTM plus multiple teacher/global/local views may be impractical on 16 GB; use a smaller paired encoder if profiling confirms this.
3. **Inspect representations before adding complexity.** Record per-lead/time mask coverage, feature variance/effective rank, view agreement for same versus different patients, and rare-morphology retention where labels allow it. For dense losses, check that masked inputs do not leak targets via correlated leads or overlapping resampling support. Positive-pair crops must retain clinically relevant segments; an aggressive short local crop can encourage invariance to a focal abnormality.
4. **Promote one objective at a time.** Compare full-label and 10% downstream AUROC, average precision, calibration/Brier, sensitivity and specificity at the existing calibration-only threshold, plus patient-bootstrap intervals. A lower SSL loss or stronger image benchmark does not establish ECG transfer. Do not choose a method using the repeatedly examined PTB-XL test set; use development data to decide whether to advance an arm and label final test results exploratory.

**Source scope:** Paper and code claims above come from the authors' papers or repositories. ECG adaptations, rankings, mask design, compute judgments, and gates are project proposals, not published claims or measured wins.
